<h1 style='text-align: center'><b>Dimension 2 — Did the average Indian become economically better off?</b></h1>

### **Central Question**
> **Did economic ouput and income per person increase, and what can we reasonably infer about changes in people's material living standards?**

#### **Questions we'll investigate**

1. **How did GDPper capita change?**
    - Did output per person grow in each period, and at what rate?

2. **How did GNI per capita change?**
    - Did income accruing to Indian residents follow the same pattern as domestic production?

3. **How much did population growth affect the results?**
    - How did population growth compare with real GDP growth?

4. **Did nominal increases translate into real gains?**
    - How does inflation change our interpretation of monetary figures?

5. **Did India's average income growth keep pace with overall economic growth?**
    - Compare real GDP growth with real GDP-per_capita growth.

6. **What can averages tell us—and what can they hide?**
    - Could average income rise while many households experience different outcomes?

#### **Data we'll need**

| **Indicator** | **Purpose** |
|---------------|-------------|
|  GDP per capita, current US$ | Nominal international-dollar comparison |
| GDP per capita, constant prices | Compare ouput per person after price adjustment |
| GNI per capita | Measure income received by residents, including net primary income from abroad |
| Population | Understand the effect of demographic growth |
| GDP deflator or suitable price index | Help interpret nominal versus real changes | 

**One important limitation:** real GDP per capita is an output measure, not a direct measure of the average person's disposable income. And even average income measures cannot reveal how income is distributed across households. We'll need other dimensions to investigate those questions.
